Thanjavur ke bahar ek ghar ke pichhe puraana kuaan tha, jo pehle se hi band tha.  Baarish ke baad uski deewar gir gayi, aur safai ke dauraan neeche se ek matka nikla — usme chhote tambe ke sikke, ek chhota deepak, aur do chhalle.  Ghar ke mard pehle khush hue, phir dare.  Do din baad.   Do din baad ek aadmi aaya jo khud ko “vaastu-shastri” kehta tha.  Usne sikke chhue, aankhein band ki, aur kaha: “Ye maal shraapit hai.  Ise ghar se nikalna hoga, warna teen peedhi tak rog.”  Nikalne ka tarika?  Use dena.  Usne teen hazaar maange.  Ghar walon ne de diye.   Is kahani mein sabse zyada dukh ki baat ye hai ki jo cheez unhe mili thi, wo asli purani thi — aur unke paas reh sakti thi, ya kam se kam ek record ban sakta tha.  Unke haath mein ek purane gaon ka hissa aaya tha, aur unhone use ek aadmi ke darr ke badle bech diya.  Purani cheez ka sahi raasta.   Zameen se nikalne wali purani cheez ek document ki tarah hoti hai.  Sikke se pata chalta hai kis saal, kis raajya mein, kis dhaatu ki tijaarat thi.  Isliye kanoon usse “khazana” nahi, “antiquity” maanta hai.  Aise maal ki kharid-farokht, aur desh se bahar bhejna, Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972 ke tehat niyamit hai.  Aur Treasure Trove Act ke tehat milne ki soochna dena zaroori hai.   Sahi raasta lamba lagta hai — thana, Collector, puratatva vibhag, kagaz, intezaar.
