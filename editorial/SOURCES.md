# Editorial references and boundaries

Consulted 23 October 2026. This is a creative storytelling edition, not a new historical investigation. Personal narrators, dialogue and sensory scenes are invented. The factual notes are deliberately narrower than the narratives. Existing image attributions are preserved from the supplied project.

- Bhangarh legends: Rajasthan Tourism, https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/content/rajasthan-tourism/en/tourist-destinations/bhangarh-fort.html — official presentation of the two legends, not proof of curses.
- Kuldhara: Rajasthan Tourism, https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/haunted-places-of-rajasthan.html — popular departure story presented as folklore. No single cause of abandonment asserted.
- Mayong: Assam Tourism, https://assamtourism.gov.in/Mayong1.php — context for the place and bez practitioners; supernatural and treatment claims are not treated as established facts.
- Narayanrao: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayan_Rao — 1773 historical context. Detailed pursuit, dialogue and inner experience are not reconstructed evidence. Conflicting tourism-page dates were not copied.
- Naale Baa: The Asian Age, 21 September 2018, https://www.asianage.com/amp/life/more-features/210918/spooky-urban-legend.html — retrospective account of the 1990s legend and doorway inscription.
- Datiyana: Times of India, 1 October 2017, https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/meerut/bhootonwala-mandir-a-temple-in-meerut-built-by-ghosts/articleshow/60898122.cms ; SBS Hindi, https://www.sbs.com.au/language/hindi/en/podcast-episode/do-you-know-about-a-temple-built-by-ghost-in-india/ohzh7r0mj — Hapur, Uttar Pradesh; temple-building belief. Removed unsupported Madhya Pradesh location and construction-century assertion.
- Muhnochwa: Times of India, 18 July 2002, https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/lucknow/muhnochwa-scare-in-ups-rural-areas/articleshow/16304788.cms — reporting of complaints, not creature verification. No blanket medical diagnosis retained.
- Pichhla Bhoot: India Today, 18 June 2016, https://www.indiatoday.in/fyi/story/pichhla-bhoot-slippery-ghost-that-gropes-women-haunts-alipurduar-villages-14914-2016-06-18 — name/context checked. The new narrative is explicitly fiction, not a retelling of that report; haircut/video assertions from the supplied draft are not verified facts.
- Monkey Man: Wired, 18 June 2001, https://www.wired.com/2001/06/monkey-business-2 — reporting of the scare and panic consequences. The family depicted here is fictional; individual complaints are not all assigned one cause.
- Black Shuck: David Owen, Journal of Religious History (2024), https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1467-9809.13099 ; Blythburgh history, https://blythburgh.onesuffolk.net/history/tales-from-blythburgh-history/a-strange-and-terrible-wonder/ — historical context for Fleming's pamphlet. A period account is not supernatural verification.

Nishi, Yakshi, Chudail, Bhulona, La Llorona and Kuchisake-onna are creative versions of the folklore themes supplied in the original project, not authenticated encounters. No exact origin date, comprehensive taxonomy or single definitive version is claimed. The warehouse riddle in Vikram–Vetala is original to this edition, explicitly identified as such.

The site's reader remains internally navigated. External reference URLs are kept here for editorial review rather than adding interface controls.
